# Lab: Prompt Engineering in Practice


# Solo + Pair Lab: "Make It, Then Check It"

## Learning Goals

When you finish this lab, you will be able to:

1. Spot common prompt mistakes and fix them.
2. Use the **5 parts of a good prompt**: Context, Task, Format, Example, Constraints.
3. Give and receive useful feedback on a prompt (peer review).
4. Break a large task into a **prompt chain**.

## How This Lab Works

| Section | Who | Time |
|---|---|---|

| **Section 2** (Parts 3, 4 & 5) | **You and a partner** (pairs of 2) | about 15 minutes |

Each student hands in **their own** document. Section 2 can be the same for both partners.

## SECTION 1: SOLO (about 15 minutes)

### Part 1: Spot the Problem (5 minutes)

For each weak prompt, name the **main mistake** and write a **better prompt**. Choose from: *Too vague*, *Assuming the AI knows your context*, *Output format not specified*, *Ignoring AI limitations*, *Overloading with information*. You do **not** need to run these prompts.

1. "Fix my laptop."
2. "Who will win the Stanley Cup next season?"
3. "Why won't it open?"
4. "Tell me about healthy breakfasts." *(The person wanted a quick list to take shopping, but got a long essay.)*
5. "Plan my 7-day trip to Japan, make a full budget, write a packing list, teach me basic Japanese phrases, and write a 1,000-word history of Tokyo."

### Part 2: Prompt Makeover (10 minutes)

Pick **one** scenario:

- **S1:** Email your professor to ask for a 2-day extension on an assignment.
- **S2:** A cheap meal plan for one week on a $60 student budget.
- **S3:** Explain what "the cloud" is to your grandparent.
- **S4:** Practise for a part-time job interview at a coffee shop.
- **S5:** A study schedule for 3 midterms next week.

Write and **run** two versions:

| Version | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Version 1 (weak)** | A short, vague, one-line prompt |
| **Version 2 (best)** | Use **all 5 parts** (Context, Task, Format, Example, Constraints), **labelled**, plus **one** technique (Role-playing, Few-shot, Chain of thought or Negative prompting). Name the technique. |

📸 Take a screenshot of the AI's answer to **both** versions.

---

## SECTION 2: PAIR (about 15 minutes)

Find a partner (pairs of 2; your instructor may form one group of 3 if needed).

### Part 3: Peer Review (6 minutes)

1. Swap your **Version 2** prompts and screenshots.
2. Review your partner's prompt using this checklist. Write ✓ or ✗ for each:

| Check | ✓ / ✗ |
|---|---|
| Context: Does it explain who they are or what situation they are in? | |
| Task: Is the action clear? | |
| Format: Does it say how the answer should look? | |
| Example: Does it give a sample? | |
| Constraints: Does it set limits (length, tone, what to avoid)? | |
| Technique: Is the named technique used correctly? | |

3. Give your partner **one specific suggestion** to make their prompt even better. Write it down.
4. Record the review **you received** in your own document.

### Part 4: Prompt Chaining Together (7 minutes)

Pick **one** big task together:
- **C1:** Start a small online business selling handmade cards
- **C2:** Prepare for your first job interview
- **C3:** Move into your first apartment

Write a **chain of 3 prompts**, where each builds on the answer before it. Run them **in the same chat**. Example (C3):

1. "List the 5 most important things to do in the month before moving into a first apartment."
2. "For item 2 from your list, give me a step-by-step checklist."
3. "Turn that checklist into a table with 3 columns: Task, Deadline, Done (✓)."

📸 Take a screenshot of the **final** answer.

### Part 5: Reflection (2 minutes, or finish after class)

On your own, answer in **2 to 3 sentences each**:

1. What did your partner's feedback teach you about your prompt?
2. Give **one** example of something you should **not** trust an AI to answer correctly, and explain why.
